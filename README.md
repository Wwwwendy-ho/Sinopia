# Sinopia

*(A sinopia is the red-earth underdrawing beneath a fresco, the first drawing that shows through the finished work. To rename the site, see "Renaming the site" below.)*

A small static web app for artists: load a photo of your drawing and the reference you worked from, then compare them side by side, stacked, or overlaid.

Plain HTML/CSS/JS (classic scripts on a `window.DrawingCompare` namespace). No build step, no backend, no dependencies.

## Privacy: your images never leave your device

The website hosts only the code. Images you load are decoded and drawn entirely in your browser; nothing is uploaded anywhere.

To survive a page reload, the app saves each slot's **shelf** of photos (up to 12 photos for the Drawing and up to 12 for the Reference, so at most 24) and each photo's view settings (mirror/flip/rotation, gray/contrast, pan/zoom, alignment and alignment dots) plus the global settings (layout, compare mode, opacity and so on) in **your browser's own IndexedDB storage**, on your device only. Each photo is stored as a PNG, JPEG or WebP: files up to 1.5 MB are kept exactly as they are, anything larger (or any other format) is re-encoded as a JPEG (quality 0.9, long side at most 2048 px; large transparent PNGs lose their transparency), which is typically 0.4 to 1 MB per photo, so a full app is on the order of 25 MB. A small thumbnail is stored with each photo. Camera shots are saved as JPEG (quality 0.92). Automatic alignment runs in a Web Worker in your browser; no image data is sent anywhere. Use **Clear all saved photos** (top bar on desktop, under the "More" button in the bottom bar on phones) to remove every photo; it tells you how many will go before it does anything.

Nothing is ever deleted without a tap: when a shelf is full (12 photos), or the browser reports little free space, or a write fails with a quota error, the app asks whether to remove the oldest photo (never the one on the easel), let you choose which to remove, or cancel (the new photo then stays on screen for this session only, is marked as not saved, and is gone after a reload). The first time a shelf reaches 2 photos the app asks the browser to make the storage persistent (`navigator.storage.persist()`); the browser decides. If the browser refuses storage altogether (private mode), the shelves live in memory for the session and the app says once that it won't restore them next time.

## Features

- **Two slots, Drawing and Reference, each with a photo shelf.** Add photos by file picker, drag and drop, clipboard paste (Ctrl/⌘+V or the Paste button) or camera capture (live preview with front/back switch; falls back to the phone's native camera via a file input when the live camera is unavailable or blocked). Every add **appends** to the slot's shelf (up to 12 photos, oldest first, in capture order) and puts the new photo on the easel; nothing is overwritten. Photos are labelled automatically ("#3 · 10 Nov 14:32"); there is no reordering or renaming.
- **Shelf stepper and strip.** Once a slot has 2 or more photos its pane header shows `‹ 3 / 5 ›`. The chevrons (or `,` older and `.` newer for the selected image) step through the photos; the count opens a strip of thumbnails (Escape, picking a photo, or tapping outside closes it; in Overlay both strips open downward under the single header). Each photo remembers its own mirror, zoom, placement and dots. A newly added or never-viewed photo of the same shape (within 2 %) lands where the previous one was, anything else is fitted. **Remove** takes away the photo on the easel (after a confirmation); the older neighbour, else the newer one, takes its place. Placement is saved per photo, not per pair: if you switch the Reference to a different photo, the Drawing keeps its own placement and may need **Align** again.
- **Layouts:** Side by side, Stacked, Overlay. **Overlay is one framed sheet:** a single canvas under one header that carries two **layer tabs**, `Drawing` and `Reference` (the tab you press is the selected image; each tab has its own eye on desktop, the selected one also shows its photo stepper), and, at the right, the tools of the selected layer only (on phones and short landscape windows they live in a tray opened from the one tools button, captioned "Drawing tools" or "Reference tools"). An empty layer shows a dashed `Reference +` tab; select it to get File, Camera and Paste. Compare mode, Swap and the one slider are a frameless **compare strip** under the sheet (on phones one 44 px row, plus a second row only for the Blend / Outline slider). In Overlay the header band is forced to one row (tagline hidden, icon-only labels at tablet widths) and the phone masthead and desk strip give their space to the picture; Side and Stacked are unchanged.
- **Compare modes (Overlay):** **Tint** (default) draws the reference in red and your drawing in blue, multiplied: where they agree it goes dark and neutral, and a red or blue fringe is exactly where you are off. **Blend** fades one image into the other (opacity slider, Swap picks which is on top). **Wipe** is a draggable curtain with your drawing on one side and the reference on the other at full strength (drag the knob, or focus it and use the arrow keys). **Outline** lays the reference's contours as a red line drawing over your untouched drawing, with a **Detail** slider. Tint and Outline colours are fixed (reference red, drawing blue) and the two swatches in the layer tabs name them. Cycle modes with `T` / `Shift+T`; `[` / `]` adjust the mode's one slider.
- **Per image:** Mirror, Flip, Rotate 90°, Grayscale, Contrast boost, Fit, Remove (the photo on the easel). **Gray all** toggles grayscale globally for value checks.
- **Navigation:** images auto-fit and centre on load. Drag to pan, scroll wheel or pinch to zoom. **Double-tap / double-click hides or shows the selected image** (a manual blink: flick between the two to spot differences; a chip names what is hidden and brings it back, and `H` or the eye button do the same; not saved, a reload shows both). The selected image is the one you last pressed (Side, Stacked) or the layer tab you pressed in Overlay (or `A`). **Link** (on by default) keeps both images in sync; turn it off to move one image alone (in Overlay, the selected tab says which one; the first time you switch Link off there a toast explains it). There is no fit gesture; see **Fit** below.
- **Fit.** **Fit all** (`F`, top bar) means "fit the pair, then line up": both images are centred at once, then the drawing is lined up with the reference. An alignment you adjusted by hand (aligned, then panned, zoomed or nudged on one image alone) is kept. Otherwise existing **dots** win (the same alignment the **Line up** flow makes, and no automatic match is run). With no dots and no hand-tuning, the same subject-aware automatic match used on load runs (an "Aligning…" chip on the canvas; pan, zoom, a layout switch or a new photo cancels it) and the drawing glides into place; the result is cached per pair of photos and orientations, so pressing it again is instant. A confident match is applied; a rough (approximate) one is only **offered** (see Auto-align below) and the placement stays as fitted; a weaker result leaves the placement unchanged and offers **Line up with dots**. Every Fit all outcome is on the **Undo align** list (see below) and restores all views exactly. **Fit** (per image, `0`) never matches: with Link on and an alignment it fits that image and the other follows, so the pair stays lined up; with Link off, or without an alignment, it fits only that image (an alignment that is dropped says so, with Undo). `Shift+F` fits every image on its own and clears the alignment. The saved per-photo view carries two extra fields, `alignSource` (`null` / `dots` / `auto`) and `tuned`; older saved views load without them.
- **Layout of controls:** each image's tools (add from file, camera, mirror, flip, rotate, gray, contrast, fit, remove photo) live in that image's own pane: in the pane header on desktop, in a slide-up tray opened from the pane header on phones. Global controls (layout, link, grid, fit all, export, clear all saved photos) sit in the top bar on desktop and in a bottom bar on phones.
- **Line up with dots (reliable, guided):** one tool, **Line up** (key `D`, in both images' tool rows), opens a four-step coach on the canvas. Step 1 and 2: tap two clear spots far apart on the **reference** (a vermilion circle, then a blue square; the reference's dots are dashed with an `R1`/`R2` tab). Step 3 and 4: tap the **same two spots** on **your drawing** (solid rings, tabs `1`/`2`). Pairs are told apart by colour *and* shape, so it works in grayscale. The app switches to the right image by itself at each step (in Overlay each step shows only the image you are marking), shows a small thumbnail of the reference spot while you mark the drawing, and after the last dot it aligns automatically (position, size and rotation) and stays open in a review state: drag any dot to fine-tune (touch drags show a magnifier), **Done**, **Undo**, **Start over**, or **Back** one dot at a time. **Use one spot** matches position only; **Cancel** (x or `Esc`) restores everything. The first time, a short picture explains "match the same two spots" (the coach's `?` shows it again). Too-close dots and a result that moved the drawing a lot get a plain-language warning. Dots are only visible while the flow is open; double-tap hiding is off while it is; pan, pinch and zoom keep working while you place dots, and a tap on the coach box moves it to the other edge. Keyboard: `Enter` drops the dot at the centre of the image, arrows move a focused dot (Shift = 10 px), `Delete` removes it, `Backspace` goes back. Dots are saved per photo; sessions saved with the old Dots toggle load cleanly.
- **Auto-align (best effort):** the **Auto** button in the Drawing pane estimates **scale and position only**: it never tilts the drawing. Rotation is fixed at 0 for every estimator (facial landmarks, round contours, silhouettes, edge matching and the sparse-paper regions), implemented in each one (a closed-form scale-and-translation least-squares fit for landmarks, a search restricted to angle 0 for the edge matcher), not by zeroing an angle afterwards. An attempt is also made the first time a drawing and a reference are both loaded, and Fit all uses the same match. It shows a calm "Aligning…" state and uses facial features, round contours or enclosed silhouettes when available. A **confident** match is applied at once. A **rough** (approximate or low-confidence) match is never applied by itself: a small panel under the images says "Rough match found" with **Apply**, **Preview** (press and hold, or hover with a mouse, to see where the drawing would land without moving it) and **Dismiss**, and stays until you act or change the images. When it cannot match, its toast offers **Line up with dots**. The setting **Allow rotation in auto-align** (More menu, off by default, saved with the other settings) lets auto-align estimate a rotation as well, capped at ±15°; a true tilt beyond the cap is declined (the search edge) rather than clamped. Rotation you apply by hand (Rotate, the Line up dots flow) is unaffected. See below for what it can and cannot do.
- **Undo align / Redo:** every alignment that moves the drawing (Auto, Fit all, Fit, Line up with dots, a retake, an accepted rough match) adds a step to a short history (12 steps). **Undo align** and **Redo** buttons sit in a slip over the bottom edge of the images (44 px targets on phones), and `Z` / `Shift+Z` do the same. Unlike the toast, they never time out: they stay until you change the placement by hand (pan, zoom, nudge, Fit one image) or change the images (a new, other or removed photo, mirror, flip or turn). Each step restores the exact previous placement of the drawing, the reference and the previous-photo ghost; gray and contrast are not part of it. A new alignment after an Undo discards the Redo steps.
- **Retake:** **Camera** or **File** in the Drawing pane, while a drawing is already loaded, is "add a photo to the shelf, inheriting placement". The new photo is matched against the photo it follows (much easier than drawing-to-reference), inherits the alignment and the dots, and may keep a small real camera tilt (rotation capped at ±10°; ±15° with **Allow rotation in auto-align** on; beyond the cap the photo sits where the old one was), and the old photo stays on the shelf one step back. **Previous** (toggle, `P`) shows the shelf entry just before the one on the easel as a ghost, for a quick compare; it is disabled when there is none. The ghost reuses the shelf's decoded image (no second copy in memory or storage). If the match is not confident the new photo simply sits where the old one was, with a hint to nudge it or re-place the dots.
- **Info bubbles:** every button has a small bubble that says what it does and shows its keyboard shortcut as key caps. It appears after a short rest of the mouse on the control, when a control gets keyboard focus (Tab), and on touch when you press and hold for about half a second (the button is then *not* pressed; a quick tap works as usual). Greyed-out buttons explain why they are disabled. Escape dismisses a bubble. The text lives in data-tip and data-key attributes in index.html (a 	itle set by script is adopted automatically, so the browser's own tooltip never doubles up).
- **Extras:** grid overlay (thirds + sixths), PNG export of the current comparison (exactly what the canvas shows: the active compare mode, a Wipe divider, a hidden image absent; no knobs, tags or chips), keyboard shortcuts (press `?`), warm paper-and-vermilion UI, touch support, responsive layout.

### Auto-align: how it works, and its limits

Everything runs locally, with no image-upload service. Small bundled face-api.js models identify facial landmarks; closed contours identify round objects and clear foreground silhouettes. See [automatic subject alignment](review/automatic-subject-alignment.md). The structural fallback works as follows. Both images are reduced to 256 px luminance rasters; the gradient magnitude is taken (so thin pencil lines survive downscaling) and normalised against its local average (so a faint sketch and a contrasty photo become comparable). A coarse search then tries every scale (0.42x to 2.4x in 9 % steps) at rotation 0 only (a retake, or the Allow rotation setting, adds rotation steps of about 4° up to the ±10° / ±15° cap, never further); for each, the best translation comes from one FFT cross-correlation. With rotation off the search is three to four times faster. The best two candidates are refined at 128 px with progressively finer scale/rotation steps, with sub-pixel translation. The search runs in a Web Worker (inline, chunked by a short delay, if workers are unavailable).

A structural result is considered confident when three checks pass: normalised correlation >= 0.40, peak-to-sidelobe ratio >= 19, and the best scale (and rotation, when allowed) is not on the edge of the searched range (which usually means the true transform is outside it).

Measured on synthetic data (a rendered 640x480 scene versus a rotated, scaled, shifted, noisy, lightened, edge-only "sketch" of it): scale error <= 0.7 %, rotation error <= 0.2°, translation error <= 1.7 px of the reference raster (mostly <= 0.5 px) over all accepted cases, and 0 of 12 unrelated image pairs accepted. **Real pencil sketches against photos are much harder** than these synthetic ones: proportions are off, shading differs, lines wobble. Those figures were measured with rotation searched; with rotation off, a drawing that is truly tilted against its reference still matches in position and size, at a lower score. Weaker structural matches (correlation >= 0.30 and peak-to-sidelobe ratio >= 14, away from the search boundary) are only offered (Apply / Preview / Dismiss), never applied by themselves. Subject-aware results (landmarks, round contours, silhouettes) are always labelled approximate unless the landmark fit is strong, so they are normally offered too. A match can still be off, and pairs without sufficient evidence are declined. It does not estimate rotations beyond its cap (none by default; turn the image with Rotate, or use the Line up dots, first), scale ratios beyond about 0.42x to 2.4x, or mirrored images (use Mirror first). When Auto declines, **use the dots**: they are the dependable method.

### Camera permission

The app asks for the camera **at most once per session**, and only from a tap (the Camera button, the Front/Back switch, or **Resume camera**), never at page load. One shared `CameraSession` owns the stream:

- Closing the camera dialog (after a capture or Cancel) does **not** stop the camera. It goes on **standby**: the preview is paused and the tracks are disabled, not stopped, so the next photo or retake within 2 minutes reopens instantly and no browser prompts again. While it is on standby a small vermilion dot marks every Camera button. Be aware that some browsers keep their camera indicator lit during standby; that is the deliberate cost of not re-prompting, and it is bounded.
- Standby ends, and the camera is really stopped, after **120 seconds** with the dialog closed, when the page is hidden for 30 seconds, and immediately on `pagehide` (closing the tab or navigating away). If the page is hidden while the dialog is open, the preview switches off at once. When you come back within 30 seconds it resumes by itself; if the camera was released (or the system interrupted it, as iOS does when Safari goes to the background) the dialog says "Camera paused" and shows a **Resume camera** button, so the request is always made from a tap.
- The chosen camera (`deviceId`) and the last Front/Back choice are remembered with the settings and requested exactly next time. If that device is gone the id is forgotten and the app silently retries once with a plain Front/Back request (normal on Safari, which issues new ids when permission is not persistent).
- The Permissions API (`navigator.permissions.query({ name: 'camera' })`) is read, where supported, without prompting. `granted`: the preview just starts. `prompt` or unknown: one line explains that the browser will ask. `denied`: the app does **not** call `getUserMedia`; on phones it opens the native camera through a file input with a toast, on desktop it opens the dialog in its error state ("allow the camera in the site settings, then press Resume camera") with a file-picker fallback. Browsers that reject the `camera` name are treated as "unknown" and behave as before.
- Captures are JPEG (quality 0.92) rather than PNG.

The app cannot make any browser remember a choice; it can only avoid asking more often than the browser requires. Whether your browser remembers the answer for next time is up to the browser: Chrome does by default on this site, Firefox and Safari need you to choose "remember" / "Allow" for the website. Permission is per origin, `https://<user>.github.io`, so it is shared by every project page of that user and survives redeploys.

| Browser | After the user allows once | Still asks again when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chrome / Edge (desktop, Android) on **https** (GitHub Pages is https) | "Allow while visiting the site" is remembered for the origin across reloads and visits; the app then never prompts again. | The user picked "Allow this time" (lasts for that visit only); the page is opened from `file://` (not remembered); site data or permissions were reset. |
| Firefox **(not verified against current Firefox documentation)** | Allowed for the current use only, unless the user ticks "Remember this decision". | Typically on a later camera request once the stream has been released (after the 2-minute standby, page hide or a reload), unless remembered. Keeping the stream on standby is what avoids prompts between retakes. |
| Safari, macOS and iOS / iPadOS **(not verified against current Safari documentation)** | Allowed for the current page load. | On each new page load or visit, unless the user sets the site to Allow (macOS: Safari > Settings for This Website > Camera; iOS: the "aA" / page menu > Website Settings > Camera). iOS also pauses the camera when Safari goes to the background; Resume camera covers that. |

The Chrome / Edge row is well-established behaviour, but none of the rows has been tested on real hardware for this app. The Firefox and Safari rows describe how those browsers are generally understood to behave and have **not** been checked against their current documentation; treat them as a starting point and check them before relying on them. Permissions API support is also uneven (older Firefox and Safari reject the `camera` name).

## Running it

**Locally:** either open `index.html` directly in a browser, or serve the folder with any static server (for example `python -m http.server` or `npx serve`) and open the printed URL. The live camera needs a secure context, which `localhost` and `file://` count as.

**GitHub Pages:**

1. Push these files to a repository (the app lives at the repo root; all paths are relative, so a project subpath such as `https://<user>.github.io/<repo>/` works).
2. In the repository go to **Settings → Pages**, choose **Deploy from a branch**, pick your branch and the `/ (root)` folder, and save.
3. After a minute the site is live at `https://<user>.github.io/<repo>/`. GitHub Pages serves over HTTPS, which the camera and clipboard APIs require.

If the site does not update after a push, open the repository's **Actions** tab and look at the latest "pages build and deployment" run. A run that failed at startup is usually a temporary GitHub outage (check githubstatus.com); use **Re-run all jobs**, or push any small commit to start a fresh build. After it succeeds, hard-reload the page (Ctrl+Shift+R) because Pages caches files for several minutes.

## Proportions check (the interface)

**Proportions** (button between *Fit all* and *More*, bottom bar on phones, key `K`) checks where each feature of the drawing sits compared with the reference and how big it is. It never opens by itself; a small vermilion dot on the button says faces were found in both images (a one-time toast offers *Check proportions*). The face finder runs when Proportions opens, and in the background shortly after an image is added or changed, cached per photo; **nothing is downloaded on page load**.

- **Layouts.** It is a check layer over Side, Stacked or Overlay. In Overlay it switches to Blend (reference at 40%) and puts the previous mode and slider back on close. On phones it always uses Overlay and the report is a bottom sheet with three heights (drag or tap the tape handle); on desktop the report is a taped column at the right and the images shrink to make room.
- **Pin and Size by.** Pin: *Auto (best fit)* (default), *Between the eyes*, *Nose*, *Chin*, *Mouth*, or drag the pin (it snaps to a feature within 12 px). Size by: *Robust auto* (default), *Head height*, *Eye line to chin*, *Eye spacing*, *Custom two points* (drag the square ends). A feature that defines the pin or the measure cannot be wrong by definition; the report says so. Nothing is turned unless *Match head tilt* is on; a tilt difference is one report item.
- **Report.** Whole-sentence differences ranked by size, numbered tags that match the canvas, arrows at true length from the drawing's point to where the reference puts it (vermilion = major, sinopia = minor), a bracket for the selected item, knock-on effects listed under their cause, a *Close enough* line, Arrows / Points / Grid toggles (the grid is a diagnostic thin-plate warp: warm stretched, blue squashed), and Tolerance *Strict / Usual / Relaxed* (no single percentage: each feature has its own limit). Jaw and chin are only ever marked *possible*. Left and right are the person's own.
- **Editing.** Drag any point (mouse), or press and hold then drag (touch, with the usual bubble and a magnifier); *Edit points* makes a plain touch drag move the nearest point. Arrow keys move a focused point by 1 px (Shift: 10). *Reset points* finds the features again.
- **No face.** *Place points myself* asks what is being compared. *A face: five points* (both eyes, nose tip, mouth centre, chin) is a coach where every step after the eyes can be skipped (*Skip*): whatever can be measured from the points placed is, and the report says which parts were not placed and are therefore not measured. *Something else: matching points* is the free-point mode below. *Compare outlines* switches Overlay to Outline. If the Face Landmarker cannot load, the basic face-api detector is used and the report says only larger differences are found.
- **Matching points (any subject).** Tap a spot on the reference, then the same spot on the drawing; up to 12 numbered pairs (the same flow is reachable from *Edit points* via *Use matching points*, and *Add points* later). The drawing is fitted to the reference by the same robust L1 fit as the face check (scale and shift, never a turn), so one badly matched pair does not drag the rest; at least 3 pairs are needed to compare. Each pair is reported as its distance from where the reference puts it, in % of the reference points' overall spread (the diagonal of their bounding box): "Point 3 6% too far left", with an arrow at true length and a numbered tag; Strict / Usual / Relaxed set the limit (4% x 0.72 / 1 / 2, major at twice the limit). Drag a point to adjust it, tap the numbered chip in the report to remove a pair; every add, move and removal is an Undo / Redo step. Left and right are as you look at the picture. Matching points need no face finder and are saved with the drawing's view (they are dropped when either photo changes). On phones the sheet drops to its smallest height while points are placed.
- **Export.** While Proportions is open, *Export* (`E`) saves the PNG with the visible marks (points, arrows, brackets, tags, pin, measure line, grid) drawn over each image pixel for pixel as on screen, plus a taped report strip under the images that lists the flagged items in the current language (numbered like the tags) and a one-line footer (size by, pin, tolerance). With Proportions closed the export is unchanged. `composeExport` takes `overlays` and `strip`; `DrawingCompare.PropUI.exportParts()` supplies them.
- **Help (?).** A small hand-drawn picture (pin, size-by line, an arrow from a spot on the drawing to the same spot on the reference, matching spots) with four whole-sentence captions in all five languages.
- **Undo.** Opening, pin, size, tilt and point edits are steps on the existing Undo / Redo list (`Z` / `Shift+Z`); the Undo bar names the last step. The alignment made on opening stays after closing; point edits leave the list on close but stay on the photo.
- **Languages.** All strings are whole-sentence templates with placeholders in `js/prop-i18n.js` (en, zh-Hans, zh-Hant, es, fr), read with `DrawingCompare.i18n.t('prop.key', { n })`; the older text-matching catalogue still handles every other string.
- Not included yet: distance ratios between linked pairs, a "mirrored drawing" warning, ears and hairline items, hands and body joints, the outline band.
## Proportions engine

`js/landmarks.js` and `js/proportions.js` are the engine of the Proportions check (interface above: `js/prop-ui.js`, `js/prop-i18n.js`, `css/proportions.css`). Details, tolerances, bias table and limits: `review/proportions-engine.md`.

- `DrawingCompare.Landmarks.detect(canvasOrImage)` finds faces and returns `{ faces: [{ points, score, box, detector }] }` in the source image's pixels. It uses MediaPipe Face Landmarker (self-hosted in `vendor/mediapipe/` and `models/face/face_landmarker.task`, Apache-2.0, about 13.5 MB once, loaded only on the first call, never on page load) and falls back to the face-api tiny model already in the app if MediaPipe cannot load.
- `DrawingCompare.Proportions` has pure functions: `features`, `align`, `measure`, `distortionGrid` (and `analyse`, which chains them). They return structured data (keys, numbers, side, arrows), not English text.
- `alignPairs(pairs)` and `measurePairs(pairs, alignment, { tolerance })` are the free-point mode: `pairs` is `[{ drawing: {x,y}, reference: {x,y} }]`; the fit is scale + shift by the same IRLS fit; every pair gets `dx`, `dy` (drawing minus reference, % of the reference points' spread), `direction` (`too_far_left`, `too_far_right`, `too_high`, `too_low`: the larger axis), severity and an arrow. `align` also accepts faces with missing nose, mouth or chin (hand-placed points with Skip): with no chin both faces are measured in units of 1.9 x the eye spacing (`eyeUnit`), items that need a missing point are skipped, and with no midline there is no head-tilt item.
- Values are corrected for the detector's bias and for how much of a drawn displacement it follows (gain). Usual tolerances: 3.5% of the eye-to-chin distance for eyes, nose length and mouth height, 6.5% mouth width, 7% chin, jaw possible only above 10%; Strict is 0.72x, Relaxed 2x.
- Left and right in the data are the person's own.
- Frontal faces only have been tested; chin and jaw measures are unreliable and are reported only as weaker findings.

## Body, hand and outline engines (phase 3, engines only; the interface is built on this API)

Manual first: drawings are compared from points the artist places (body coach, 7-point hand coach); references are auto-detected (Pose Landmarker full, lite fallback, crop retry; Hand Landmarker) and can be corrected by hand. Detection on the drawing is only ever offered. Method, tolerances, held-out numbers and limits: `review/proportions-body-hands-outline.md`. All engines return keys and numbers (no English), left and right are the person's own, and nothing is downloaded on page load (each model loads when its mode is first used).

- `DrawingCompare.Landmarks.pose(img, { model, crop, cropFirst, maxSide, numPoses })` -> `{ poses: [{ points[33] {x,y,z,visibility}, world[33], box, score }], model: 'full'|'lite', ms, retried, error? }`; `.hands(img, { crop, numHands })` -> `{ hands: [{ points[21], world[21], label, score, box }], ms }`; `.segment(img, { maxSide })` -> `{ mask (1 = person), width, height, kx, ky, ms }`; `.loadTask('pose'|'hand'|'segment')`, `.taskStatus(task)`, `.onTaskStatus(fn)`, `.resetTask()`. Points are in the source image's pixels. CPU delegate, no pose segmentation masks.
- `DrawingCompare.BodyProportions`: `fromPoints(named, { foreshortened })`, `fromPose(pose)`, `align(D, R, { size: auto|head|torso|shoulders|height|custom, pin: auto|neck|hips|foot|{x,y}, matchTilt })`, `measure(D, R, alignment, { tolerance, tapNoise, kappa })`, `analyse`, `mirrorCheck`. Items: height, legs, torso, shoulder and hip width, 8 segments, 8 limb angles, reach, shoulder and hip tilt, contrapposto, lean, gesture line, balance, joint placement; heads for figure-scale lengths, % for single segments, degrees for angles; `groups` hold knock-on effects, `warnings` the foreshortening, balance and unit notes.
- `DrawingCompare.HandProportions`: `COACH`, `fromPoints`, `fromLandmarks(hand, { side })`, `align`, `measure`, `analyse`. Items: finger lengths (% of that finger), thumb length and placement, knuckle line, palm width, finger spread, bend, hand angle, hand size (when sized from outside).
- `DrawingCompare.Outline`: `grayFromImage`, `maskFromSegment`, `lineMap`, `lineRoute(gray, { seed })`, `contour(mask)`, `compare(contour, lines, alignment, { tolerance, bodyPoints })`, `analyse({ reference, drawing, alignment, bodyPoints, seed })` -> bands (polylines), regions named by body part or position, items "outline N% too far out / in" in % of the subject's height, notes for stretches that could not be followed.
- `DrawingCompare.ProportionsMode.choose({ drawing, reference }, { override })` -> `{ mode, auto, need, offer, pending, sections, models, reasons, notes }`.
- Tolerances are the larger of a floor and `kappa` x the expected tap or detector noise of that item (`tapNoise`, in head heights or palm lengths); a tap placed with the zoom lens is assumed by default. The tables in the review document show what that detects.

## Browser support
Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari (desktop and mobile). Grayscale/contrast use the Canvas `filter` property where available and fall back to per-pixel processing elsewhere.

## Renaming the site

Edit the two strings in `js/brand.js` (`SITE.name` and `SITE.slug`, the latter is the export file-name prefix). The static `<title>` and the two pre-script placeholders in `index.html` (`data-site-name`) only matter for no-JS and link previews; update them too if you care. Do **not** rename `DB_NAME` in `js/storage.js`: it is a storage key, and changing it hides every saved photo.


### Visual identity

`icons/logo.svg` is the transparent vector head study: a strong charcoal silhouette with red-earth construction arcs. `icons/icon-source.svg` is the opaque square home-screen master. Apple touch (180 px), app (192/512 px), and maskable (512 px with extra safe-area inset) PNGs share the same mark. The favicon simplifies the interior details for 16–32 px. The website uses the same vector contours beside the name. Devices apply their own home-screen corner masks.

### Interaction sounds

Quiet, browser-generated studio foley accompanies frequent controls: wooden-track slide for layouts, dry graphite for grid/value/compare tools, paper for fitting/flipping/shelf navigation, a soft contact tap for Link/Hide, and eraser brushing after successful photo removal. No audio is downloaded or played on page load or during pan/zoom. Disable **Interaction sounds** in Help; the choice is remembered locally. Audio support or storage failures never block tools. `js/sound.js` owns synthesis, volume, event routing and the preference.

### Proportions robustness (v2)

The check allows more room on stylised drawings (Drawing style: Auto / Realistic / Stylised), warns when a head is turned well away from front-on, skips the nose, mouth or chin when they are not drawn yet, offers to flip a drawing that looks mirrored (one Undo step), and picks the face that matches the other picture when there are several. `js/prop-ink.js` holds the pixel measures (ink, noise, faint-drawing stretch). Method, tables and held-out numbers: `review/proportions-engine.md`.
